## The conflict that stemmed from a simple misunderstanding
I had made it to the Finals for the Cheonan City 2026 National Youth English Speech Contest out of the 10 middle school finalists. Since I now had an idea of what it would feel like to get on stage, my mind didn't go over the worst-case scenarios. This competition would be one that I would get to enjoy. 

However, before the day of the competition, my parents and I faced a conflict.


Arguments are something that every family experiences, and it's normal to clash with your parents. I'm on the friendlier side with my parents, so we don't have trouble exchanging conversations or opinions. However, a week before my competition, they were mad. Really mad.

In their eyes, I was a 13-year-old teenager who wasn't enthusiastic about public speaking and didn't want to practice for the contest that was a week away. Every time I would practice by recording a video of myself presenting the speech on my dad's phone—originally, it was his idea—my parents only saw my tired expression. They thought I didn't care about this opportunity at all.

The day they expressed their anger, they had told me to let them know when my club meeting was over so that they could record the practice video. I agreed, but the club meeting went on for much longer than I had thought it would. As we went over everything that needed to be discussed, I ended up forgetting what my parents had told me to do. I was focused on finishing the club work that had been assigned to me instead of the task my parents asked me to do. This was my fault since I should have told my parents right away after the meeting finished or written a note reminding myself to tell them if I didn't feel confident about remembering to do so.

My parents viewed this behavior as a passionate dislike for public speaking and an act of giving up when the contest was only a few days away.

I can see why they would think that, but that wasn't the truth. Not at all.

The disengaged look on my face during practice wasn't a sign of my hatred; it was frustration. I was frustrated with myself. As the videos progressed, I couldn't see any noticeable improvement. I cared *so* much. My parents thought I didn't.

Additionally, the day of the club meeting, I wasn't trying to avoid practice. In fact, I genuinely wanted to do the practice video. After the meeting, I wanted to finish the club work assigned to me quickly as it wasn't that difficult to complete. At that point, I forgot all about what my parents had told me to do, and I got carried away by another assignment that I was so close to finishing. Then, they saw what I was doing. I didn't realize what was wrong. I even asked my dad to record the video with his phone, but he had gone to his bedroom. As I was about to close the door to the room and practice by myself with my own phone, his patience just burst. He didn't realize that I wanted to practice, and I didn't realize that I hadn't kept my word.

While I was going to sleep, I finally remembered what my parents said to me.

The next day, we sat down and had a discussion. I understood their perspective, and hopefully, they understood mine as well. After this conflict, I stopped recording with my dad's phone and started using mine, which was more efficient.

### Competition Day
Behind the scenes, I worked diligently and practiced consistently. I noticed areas I needed to improve on, and despite how frustrated I was before with how I was doing, I tried to be more patient with myself. Gradually, I got better, I didn't forget my lines, and many of my unhelpful speaking habits appeared less frequently.

By the competition day, all the practice I had done gave me more confidence. As the last presenter out of 40 students, my objective was to be memorable—I would have to try to get their attention, especially since they may have lost focus after listening to the first 39 speakers.

Once all of our speeches had been presented, the award ceremony took place. I went home with an Excellence Award (우수상), placing second out of the middle schoolers.

Overall, I believe I did much better than the first contest, and seeing how proud my parents were was very fulfilling. We may have faced a disagreement, but through a calm conversation, we were able to untangle our misunderstanding. I'm very glad we had that talk, and I feel very grateful to have parents who care enough to share their side of the story and encourage me to do better.
